Research & data

Kerala had no clean dataset. We rebuilt one from archived PDFs, Assembly answers, six newsrooms and two languages.

◆ Research & depth · 30% of score
Field research · the data problem

We asked the officers first. The files stayed locked.

01 · WE ASKED

Police and Excise officers

We approached officers in the sector for case files, the ideal training data: dated, located, verified seizures.

02 · THE ANSWER

Not in time. Not without approval.

There was not enough time, and the law protects these files: they involve ongoing court cases and personal details, and need official approval to share.

03 · SO WE BUILT IT

Everything from the public record

Archived Excise statements, an Assembly answer and 628,964 news stories in two languages. Every number here can be checked by anyone.

The upside: NarcoTrace proves the method on data anyone can audit, and it is built to take official records the day they are shared.

Next month's cases for all 14 districts, with ranges.

It looks at three things: each district's usual share of Kerala's cases, the latest state total, and the time of year.

Forecast 2 · Down to one square kilometre

Which square kilometre, in the next four weeks?

We cut Kerala into 46,057 squares, each 1 km wide. Every week the computer picks the 1% most likely to see a drug seizure in the next four weeks.

Our big idea: learn where seizures get reported, not just where drugs move. That alone lifted us from 63% to 85%.

Innovation

Five things nobody had built for Kerala.

01

It knows how news works

It learns where a seizure gets reported, not just where drugs move. That took us from 63% to 85%.

02

Reads Malayalam news

It finds where a seizure happened and ignores the accused's hometown. It found 628 seizures English news missed.

03

Marks its own homework

Every weekly list is locked, then checked four weeks later. If the new version does worse than the old one, it switches back by itself.

04

Tips that can't be misused

What and roughly where, never who. An area shows only when 5 or more different people report it on 2 or more days.

How it's used

A loop that checks itself every week.

Enforcement analysts
The four-week priority list, why each place, rising areas and the official district picture. They decide. Nothing is dispatched automatically.
Health & recovery planners
Drug-affected areas far from help, ranked localities and district gaps, showing where a new centre or outreach reaches the most people.
The public
Nearest help from any spot, helplines, and a safe tip form in English or Malayalam.
Built to do no harm

Safeguards are in the code, not just the slides.

PLACES, NOT PEOPLE

No names. No houses.

Tips have no person, phone or vehicle fields. Notes are scrubbed on the server. Taps snap to 1 km before they leave the phone.

ANTI-CAMPAIGN

5 people · 2 days

A sudden flood of tips is held for review. One person reporting many areas is ignored. An area must stand out from its neighbours.

ANTI-STIGMA

No public tip map

Notes describing people by faith, caste or origin are held out. Enforcement sees only dashed, unverified 3 × 3 km blocks.

NO FEEDBACK LOOP

Tips never teach the AI

Areas with fewer than five reports stay hidden. Every forecast shows how fresh its data is.

Emergencies go straight to official lines: 112 · Yodhav 99959 66666 · Excise 155358 · de-addiction 14446.
